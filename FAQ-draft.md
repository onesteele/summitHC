**22. What happens if the platform goes down during volatile markets?**
Summit's platform is a charting and order-entry interface — it isn't an exchange. When you trade a CFD you open a contract whose price tracks an underlying market (for example the NASDAQ index or a U.S. stock); the position is held with Summit as counterparty rather than routed onto that exchange. Your ability to open or close is tied to the underlying market's hours and conditions — so if the underlying exchange triggers a circuit-breaker halt, pricing for that instrument pauses for everyone until the exchange reopens it, exactly as it would for any market participant. _[If any orders are passed to an external liquidity provider/venue, describe that here.]_

## Account & getting started
**27. Do you offer a demo account before I trade with real money?**
Yes. You can use a demo account to get acclimated to the platform and practice your trading before funding a live account.

**28. What's the difference between demo and live trading?**
Demo uses paper money — a simulated balance rather than real funds — but it gives you full access to our platform and software and exposes you to real market conditions.

**29. What products can I trade, and what are the risks?**
Summit offers **CFDs** on a broad range of underlyings: indices (e.g., NASDAQ), individual stocks, commodities, metals, and cryptocurrencies. (CFDs only — Summit does not offer futures.)
